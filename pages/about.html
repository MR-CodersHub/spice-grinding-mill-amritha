We have never modernised the mill into something else. The stones are the same family of granite, the runner gap is the same, and the batch card that leaves with every pack still has to be filled in by hand before the lid is closed.

HOW IT STARTED

A Mill, a Hand-Crank, and a Very Short Supply Chain

In 1948 Amritha Menon took a lease on two rooms behind Bazaar Road and set up a hand-crank chakki bought second-hand from a decommissioned rice mill in Palakkad. He ground for the spice traders on the lane, and he ground slowly, because the stones were old and the bearings were worn.

What he did not do was blend, extend or cut. Traders brought clean pepper, he ground it, and it went back out in sacks. Seventy-eight years later the mill is climate-controlled and the grind is measured to a micron — but the deal with the customer has not changed. You bring the spice, we do not adulterate it, and the batch card tells you who grew it.

His grandson Ravi runs the stones now. His daughter Lakshmi Amritha runs the contracts with the 2,400 farming families the mill buys from, most of them in Salem, Guntur, Ramganj Mandi, Unjha and the Idukki highlands.

  • No brokers in the chain. We buy direct, and we pay the same day the lot is accepted.
  • Every pack carries its batch card. District, grower, harvest window, grind date and measured temperature.
  • Decisions are logged. If a run goes over 38 °C it is reground, not shipped.

WHERE THE SPICE COMES FROM

Five Growing Regions, One Set of Buyers

We buy direct from 2,400 farming families across five regions. Not through aggregators, not through agents who cannot tell you which field a sack came out of. Our field team visits every cluster between harvest and contract, and the growers have our direct number.
